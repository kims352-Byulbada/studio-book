First Frost

Since 1964,
Hamilton, Ontario

Some brands have to be pushed into autumn. Tim Hortons is already there. It is the red cup in a gloved hand at a bus stop, the drive-through line with the heater on, the box of Timbits on the back seat of a car on the way to a hockey rink at six in the morning. The coffee is not the point. The point is the order, said the same way every morning: a medium Double-Double, two creams and two sugars, no need to explain. It is a small ritual that tells you the day has started and that it will be fine. First Frost takes that ritual and gives it something to hold onto when the weather turns. The name comes from the first morning of the year when the windshield is white and the grass crunches. It is the morning everyone at the counter is wearing a toque for the first time, and the morning the cup feels warmer than it did a week ago. The colours come straight from the brand: Tim Hortons red, fading into the orange of a maple leaf just before it falls. Everything else stays quiet: black, cream, and plain, honest type that you read at a glance.

The collection is four pieces, made for the walk between the car and the door. Nothing is precious. Everything is meant to be used every day until spring and then put away for next year. The toque is knit in Tim Hortons red with a cream cuff, plain except for the wordmark stitched on the fold. It is the hat you grab without looking. The travel mug holds a medium and keeps it hot for the length of a commute; the lid clicks shut, so it can ride in a cup holder over a pothole without a drop on the seat. The Timbits box is printed with falling leaves in red and orange. It is the box that gets passed down a row of bleachers at a Saturday morning game. The cup sleeve is the smallest piece and the one most people will see: a band of cream card around the paper cup, with the waves from the next page printed all the way round.
